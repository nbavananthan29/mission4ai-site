The guardrails are the product

Anything touching medicines has a hard line at clinical advice, and a phone agent that wanders over it is a liability rather than a saving. This is the part most AI agencies can't build, because they don't know where the line is.

The agent takes requests and routes them. It does not counsel, it does not interpret symptoms, and it does not decide anything a pharmacist should be deciding.

What that means in practice. Ask it whether your prescription is ready and it tells you. Ask it whether you can take two of them, and it puts you through to a person, every time, without trying to be helpful first.

The clinic diary

Rings patients ahead of their appointment, confirms it, and when someone can't make it, offers the slot to the next person on your waiting list before the day is lost.

What we'll want to know before saying yes

  • What your patient management system is, and whether anything can be written into it.
  • Who is legally the caller when the agent rings a patient: you, or us.
  • Where your line sits on what the agent may say. We'll have a view; yours decides it.
  • What happens to a call the agent can't handle at two in the morning.

If the answers mean this shouldn't be built, we'll say so on the first call rather than three weeks in.
