Why this is the sensible thing to buy first

Committing to a monthly fee for a phone agent means trusting that it'll work before you've seen it work. A reactivation campaign is the other way round. It's bounded, it's priced once, and at the end of it you have a count of appointments that either justifies the next conversation or doesn't.

It's also the fastest thing we do. There's no traffic to build and no advertising to run, because the list already exists and you already paid to acquire it.

How a campaign runs

  1. You export the list

    Whoever enquired and never converted, with whatever detail you hold. We'll tell you what's usable and what isn't.

  2. We screen it before anything is dialled

    Numbers get checked against the Telephone Preference Service and its corporate equivalent, and anything that shouldn't be called is removed. This happens before the campaign, not after a complaint.

  3. We agree what the agent says

    Including who it says it's calling on behalf of. That's a question with a legal answer, and we settle it in writing before the first call.

  4. It calls, and it books

    Anyone who's still interested gets booked into your diary. Anyone who isn't gets recorded as such, so you never pay to call them twice.

  5. You get the numbers

    Called, reached, interested, booked. Plus the recordings, so you can hear what your old enquiries actually said about why they didn't buy.

The part most people skip

Calling a list is not the same as answering a phone, legally. Outbound to consumers in the UK is governed, screening against the preference services is not optional, and it matters whose data it is and who counts as the caller.

Calling your own dormant enquirers, people who came to you and gave you their number, is far safer ground than cold outreach. It still needs doing properly.

What that means for you. We won't run a campaign against a list you can't tell us the origin of. If that rules yours out, better to know now than after somebody complains.
