What it takes off the desk

Availability

Rings or answers, asks which days and which sites, and writes it straight into your system while the candidate is still on the phone. No voicemail, no callback list.

Shift confirmations

Confirms the booking, reads back the site, the time and the rate, and flags anyone who hesitates so a consultant can ring before it becomes a no-show at 7am.

Compliance chasing

Calls the people whose documents are expiring, tells them exactly which one and by when, and keeps calling the ones who don't respond. The job nobody in the office wants.

The switchboard

Answers the general line at 6am, at 10pm and on a bank holiday. Routes anything that genuinely needs a person, takes a proper message from anything that doesn't.

The arithmetic on a small desk

Take a consultant on £30,000. If a third of the working day goes on availability calls, confirmations and document chasing, that's roughly £10,000 a year of salary spent on work that fills nothing.

Put four consultants on the same desk and you're at £40,000. The agent costs a fraction of one of them and doesn't stop at half past five.

The number that matters more. An unfilled shift isn't a cost, it's revenue that never existed. If the agent catches one candidate a week who'd otherwise have gone to voicemail and taken a shift elsewhere, it's paid for itself several times over before you count the salary.

What it will not do

Worth saying plainly, because most of this industry doesn't.

  • It doesn't negotiate rates. Anything about money goes to a human, every time.
  • It doesn't make a compliance decision. It chases the document and records what came back. Whether that's acceptable is your consultant's call, not a language model's.
  • It doesn't pretend to be a person. If a candidate asks, it tells them.
  • It doesn't replace your consultants. It removes the part of their day that doesn't fill jobs.
